Seven aircraft lost GPS near Schiphol amid suspected jamming from the North Sea

The Ministry of Infrastructure and Water Management confirmed on Sunday the disruptions of Monday evening and said aviation safety was not at stake. It is the second such incident in four months, according to NRC, and the inspectorate for digital infrastructure is investigating.

16 November 2026

Seven aircraft approaching Schiphol lost their GPS signal on Monday evening between 18:15 and 18:30 and were hindered while landing, the Ministry of Infrastructure and Water Management confirmed on Sunday. Air traffic control called deliberate jamming plausible. The ministry said aviation safety was not at stake, as the aircraft could land safely on backup systems.

Air traffic control described jamming as the deliberate interference with navigation signals. It was the second such large-scale event in the Netherlands in four months, according to NRC. The Dutch aviation authority reports that jamming reports have risen since 2024 to several hundreds per month, mostly involving Dutch airlines affected abroad.

A KLM pilot reported to the Schiphol tower at 18:19 on 9 November that both of his GPS systems had failed shortly before landing. A Lufthansa pilot and an Air France pilot reported losing GPS shortly afterwards, according to reports by HLN and NRC. Skeyes, the Belgian air traffic control authority, received no reports that evening, spokesman Kurt Verwilligen said.

A report of the disruption has gone to the Rijksinspectie Digitale Infrastructuur, the inspectorate for digital infrastructure, which is now investigating. Research by the newspaper NRC, partly based on data from the American analyst John Wiseman, points to jamming, possibly from a ship, as the most plausible cause. Wiseman, who collects aircraft navigation signals, located the source on the North Sea north of Texel. At the time, six ships under Russian flag were sailing in that area. Three of them, the Lady D, the Lady R and the Maia-1, formed a convoy for Russian arms transport, according to shipping news sites, and are on the European sanctions list. The three were escorted by the Russian navy through the North Sea.

A similar episode was recorded on 23 July 2026. Jamming lasting eighteen minutes affected tens of aircraft near the border of the United Kingdom and the Netherlands, the monitoring account Hexbird reported. NATO ships, among them a Danish navy vessel and a French warship, shadowed the suspected Russian convoy.

Sources

NRC, HLN, NU.nl, RFI, RD
