Ten JEF nations, including the Netherlands, sign naval partnership pact

The agreement commits the ten navies to interchangeable equipment, parts, ammunition and personnel, with command from Northwood in London. A Russian senator called the plan a provocation.

9 November 2026

Ten countries of the Joint Expeditionary Force (JEF), whose members include the Netherlands, Denmark, Norway and the United Kingdom, will sign an agreement on November 10 to partner their naval forces. Under the pact, as agreed in July, the navies commit to aligning doctrine, training, planning, sustainment and capability development so they can substitute, swap or mix equipment, parts, ammunition or personnel, to deter Russia from military aggression in the region. Andrei Denisov, first deputy chairman of the foreign affairs committee of Russia's Federation Council, called the plan a provocation and said the impulse came from Britain.

First Sea Lord General Gwyn Jenkins said the joint forces would be commanded from Northwood maritime headquarters in London. He described the grouping as a complement to NATO able to respond to crises faster. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre called the initiative a direct response to US calls for other countries to assume more responsibility for Allied security. The United States itself stays outside the pact; Canadian participation is being considered.

On 9 July 2026, all ten JEF nations formally agreed to the Maritime Proposition, the naval partnership signed today. The move follows reporting by El País in July that Washington had informed European allies it would cut the fighter aircraft it made available to NATO in a crisis by a third and strategic bombers by half. It said Washington would also no longer provide submarines.

Norway proposed the initiative to establish a broad maritime partnership. Tore O. Sandvik, the Norwegian Defence Minister, said Europe and Canada are taking greater responsibility for maritime security and promoting more equitable burden-sharing in NATO. The result, he said, will be a stronger Europe and a stronger NATO. Jenkins said in July 2026 that the strengthened maritime cooperation enhances deterrence along the sea border with Russia and supports NATO.

In October, Canada applied to join the JEF Framework, and the defence ministers, meeting alongside Canadian Defence Minister McGuinty, worked towards a joint recommendation on the application. Iceland, for its part, will host the next JEF Leaders' Summit in Reykjavik in spring 2027.
